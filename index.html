    How to play

    The board

    The board is a big tic-tac-toe grid. Each of its nine big squares holds a small tic-tac-toe board.

    123 456 789

    Squares are numbered 1 to 9, row by row. Big squares and small squares use the same numbers. A move is written as two numbers: 4-1 means big square 4, small square 1.

    Taking turns

    X goes first and can play anywhere. After that, the small square you pick decides where the other player goes next. If you play in small square 1, they must play in big square 1.

    X plays 4-1, the top left small square of big square 4. O must now play in big square 1.

    The board shows where you can play. Those big squares are outlined in the colour of the player to move, and the rest are dimmed.

    Winning a big square

    Get three in a row on a small board to win that big square. A won square is closed, so nobody can play in it again.

    If a small board fills up with no winner, it is a tie. The Ties setting decides what happens to it.

    • Reset square. The board is wiped clean and stays in play.
    • Stays closed. The board stays as it is and is closed for the rest of the game. It counts for nobody.

    Free moves

    If you are sent to a big square that is closed, you get a free move. A square is closed when it has been won, or when it is tied and ties stay closed. The Free move setting decides where the free move can go.

    • Anywhere. You can play in any open big square.
    • Touching squares. You can only play in open big squares that touch the one you were sent to, diagonals included. If none of them is open, you can play in any open big square.

    You can change the Free move setting during a game.

    Winning the game

    Win three big squares in a row. If no open big square is left and nobody has three in a row, the game is a draw.

    Clock

    With the clock on, each player gets the chosen number of minutes for the whole game. Only the player to move counts down, and the clock switches by itself when a move is made. The clocks start once the first move has been played.

    An increment adds that many seconds to your clock after each move you make. If your time runs out, you lose. Pause stops both clocks and locks the board until you resume.

    The game also pauses when you open this page, switch to another tab or reload. Undo puts both clocks back to where they were.

    Starting a new game

    Opponent, side, Ties and Clock are fixed for a game. Changing one of them starts a new game.

    Playing the computer

    Set Opponent to Computer, then pick a level and a side. X moves first. Undo takes back your last move and the computer's reply.

    Keyboard

    Use the arrow keys to move around the board. Press Enter or Space to play in the square you are on.
